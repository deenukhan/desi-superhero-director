# Characters and sheets

Ready-to-paste sheet prompts for every character, animal, vehicle and location, the QA checks each sheet must pass before any video is generated, and the face options when the user is the hero.

## 1. Sheet rules

- **Tools:** Nano Banana Pro (about 2 credits per image) or GPT Image 2.6 (about 1 credit), on Higgsfield or any image platform. One image per sheet.
- **One character per sheet.** Never two people, never a group shot.
- **Plain warm grey studio background, soft even studio light**, photorealistic, **4 views**: front full body, side full body, back full body, and a close-up (face, head, or the key detail).
- **Every person is "an original fictional…, not a real person or celebrity".** Every sheet ends **"No text."**
- **The hero's costume goes on a faceless mannequin.** The face comes from a separate reference, so the costume sheet never fights it.
- **Distinct silhouette and colour per character.** At thumbnail size no two characters may be confused.
- **Check every sheet** (§4) before any video. A sheet reroll costs 1–2 credits; a broken video costs 20–100+. Never "fix it in the clip".
- Only make sheets for characters and props that matter. Big simple objects (a tub, a cart, a rope) can be carried by words.

## 2. Face options when the user is the hero

**Never invent a face for a real person.** Use their real photo or a sheet made from it. AI-invented likenesses get rejected.

| Option | What shows | Good for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Face visible** | Whole face, a costume without a mask (or a mask pushed up) | "Make me the hero": people recognise you | Most face drift; keep the face at medium distance; check likeness every clip |
| **Half-mask (default)** | A cap or mask down to the nose; eyes, mouth and jaw visible | Deadpan, mouth gags, a beard as an identity anchor | Recognisable to people who know you |
| **Full mask** | Nothing but eyes | Total anonymity; the easiest consistency | You're not recognisable; deadpan and mouth gags are impossible |

**Ask:** "Face visible, half-mask (mouth and jaw free) or full mask?" Default to half-mask.

**Consent:** any other real person (a friend, a parent, a child) needs their consent, and a child's needs a parent's. Without consent, make an original fictional character instead. Never use a celebrity's or public figure's face.

**Lock the build and age** in every prompt ("sturdy broad build, mid-30s, not lean"): models tend to make heroes leaner and older.

## 3. Templates

Fill every `[BRACKET]`. Keep the rest word for word.

### 3.1 Hero costume sheet (faceless mannequin)
> A highly realistic costume reference sheet on a plain warm grey studio background, soft even studio light, photographed like a film wardrobe test. A homemade Indian [village / small-town] [ARCHETYPE WORDS, e.g. bat-themed vigilante] costume on a faceless matte grey tailor's mannequin with no facial features. Show four views: front full body, side full body, back full body, and a close-up inset of the headpiece. The costume: [HEADPIECE, e.g. a black woollen knit cap pulled down to nose level with two hand-cut eye holes with frayed edges and two stiff pointed ears hand-stitched on, slightly uneven], leaving the mouth and jaw area open; [CAPE / OUTER LAYER, e.g. an old bedsheet cape with a ragged hand-torn hem, pinned at the throat with one big steel safety pin]; [TOP, e.g. a plain cotton kurta with rolled sleeves in HERO COLOUR]; on the chest [EMBLEM, e.g. a bat shape] cut from [cloth / cardboard / tin] by an amateur with scissors, one side visibly cut short, a crooked notch in the other side, wonky jagged edges, sewn on with thick [contrast colour] stitches, clearly asymmetric and homemade; [BELT, e.g. a cotton gamchha tied as a belt]; [BOTTOM, e.g. a checked lungi folded at the knee]; [FEET, e.g. blue-strap rubber chappals]. No printed logo, no oval emblem, no symmetric emblem, no official superhero colours or symbols, no text, no human face.

### 3.2 Hero face

**A. The user's real photo (simplest).** Upload one clear, well-lit, front-facing photo (shoulders up, no sunglasses, neutral background) as the first reference. No sheet needed.

**B. A six-panel identity sheet made from their photo** (better angles, better consistency). Attach the photo, then:
> Using the attached photo as the only identity reference, create a highly realistic six-panel character reference sheet of exactly this same person on a plain warm grey studio background, soft even studio light, photorealistic skin texture. Keep the face, skin tone, hair, [beard / moustache], age and build identical to the photo; do not beautify, slim or age the face. Panels: 1 face front close-up, 2 face three-quarter left, 3 face profile right, 4 face three-quarter right looking slightly up, 5 full body front in plain [neutral clothing], 6 full body side. Same person in every panel, neutral expression in 1–4. [Build: e.g. sturdy broad build, mid-30s.] No text, no labels, no logos.

Check the likeness against the photo (§4) before using it. If it drifts, use option A.

### 3.3 Villain
> A highly realistic character reference sheet on a plain warm grey studio background, soft even studio light, photorealistic skin texture. An original fictional Indian [village / small-town] [VILLAIN MOTIF, e.g. fireworks showman and kingpin], about [AGE], [BUILD, e.g. tall and lanky], [HAIR], [FACIAL HAIR], [EXPRESSION, e.g. a cocky grin with one gold tooth]. He wears [OUTFIT IN 2–3 SPECIFIC DESI PIECES], [GAG PROPERTY, e.g. a bandolier of unlit colourful paper rockets across his chest], [ONE ACCESSORY]. Show four views: front full body, side full body, back full body, and a face close-up. One person only. Not a real person or celebrity. Not a clown and not any comic-book or movie villain: no face paint, no white face, no smeared red mouth, no dark eye make-up, no green hair, no playing cards, no breathing mask, no tubes, no mouth cover, no armour plates. No text.

### 3.4 Henchman
> A highly realistic character reference sheet on a plain warm grey studio background, soft even studio light, photorealistic. An original fictional [skinny nervous / huge slow] Indian small-town henchman, about [AGE], [HEIGHT/BUILD], [HAIR], [FACIAL HAIR], [EXPRESSION]. He wears [OUTFIT, one loud colour that no other character uses], and holds [ONE BIG READABLE PROP, e.g. a long bamboo lathi with black tape on the grip]. Show four views: front full body holding the prop, side full body, back full body, and a face close-up. One person only. Not a real person or celebrity. No text.

### 3.5 Sidekick (child)
> A highly realistic character reference sheet on a plain warm grey studio background, soft even studio light, photorealistic. An original fictional Indian village [boy / girl], about [AGE 8–11], [FACE: e.g. round cheeks, a big gap-toothed grin, bright eager eyes], [HAIR]. [He / She] wears [SIMPLE EVERYDAY CLOTHES], with [HOMEMADE FAN ITEM, e.g. a plain towel tied around the neck as a cape with a wooden clothes peg]. Show four views: front full body, side full body, back full body, and a face close-up. One child only, smiling. Not a real person. No text, no logos, no emblem.

*Child rule:* the child only runs, cheers, gasps and watches. Never grabbed, thrown, carried off, hurt, near fire, near a weapon, or crying in fear.

### 3.6 Elder
> A highly realistic character reference sheet on a plain warm grey studio background, soft even studio light, photorealistic wrinkled skin. An original fictional tiny Indian [grandmother / grandfather], about [AGE 65–80], [POSTURE: slightly hunched but feisty], [HAIR], [GLASSES?], a fierce, unimpressed expression. [She / He] wears [e.g. a faded cotton saree with a contrasting border, the pallu tucked firmly at the waist, glass bangles] and holds [ONE PROP: a rolling pin / a walking stick / one rubber chappal]. Show four views: front full body, side full body, back full body, and a face close-up. One person only. Not a real person or celebrity. No text.

*Elder rule:* the elder is always in control: rides, swats, throws, blesses. Never thrown, knocked down or hurt.

### 3.7 Animal (toy or ally)
> A highly realistic animal reference sheet on a plain warm grey studio background, soft even studio light, photorealistic. A [SIZE, COLOUR] Indian [ANIMAL: camel / water buffalo / donkey / bullock] with [DISTINCT FEATURES, e.g. curled horns]. [BRANDING ORNAMENT, e.g. the left horn wrapped with a strip of checked cloth / a brass bell on a coloured cord]. [TACK: rope halter, saddle bags, none]. A calm, stubborn, unimpressed expression. Show four views: front full body, side profile full body, rear three-quarter, and a head close-up. One animal only, exactly four legs [and two horns]. No people, no text.

### 3.8 Vehicle
> A highly realistic vehicle reference sheet on a plain warm grey studio background, soft even studio light, photorealistic. An old weathered [COLOUR] Indian [VEHICLE: tractor with a hay trolley / scooter with a sidecar / handcart / water tanker / school van], unbranded, with [3 WEAR DETAILS, e.g. rusty mudguards, mud-caked tyres, patched seat] and [BRANDING ORNAMENT, e.g. a marigold garland and a small black nazar charm on the front]. Show four views: front three-quarter, full side profile, rear, and top-down. No people, no driver. No brand name, no badges, no number plate, no text anywhere.

### 3.9 Location plate (set reference)
> A highly realistic cinematic film still of an empty [LOCATION, e.g. narrow North Indian small-town galli] at [TIME OF DAY], looking [DIRECTION, e.g. down the lane toward a low sun at the far end], [LIGHT, e.g. backlit drifting dust], anamorphic lens, [GRADE]. On the left [OBJECT + exact height/position]; in the centre [OBJECT]; on the right [OBJECT]; [BACKGROUND: mud-plastered walls, tangled wires overhead]. No people, no animals, no text, no signboards. 16:9.

- Lock the **geography** (left / centre / right) in the plate and use the same positions in every shot.
- In the clip prompt declare it "a set reference only, not the opening frame". If it freezes the camera, drop it and let the look paragraph carry the set.
- Reuse plates across episodes: a reused set costs nothing and builds the world.

### 3.10 Look still (after the first good render)
Crop a clean frame of the hero from the first approved clip (hero only, full costume visible). Upload it as the **last** reference in every later clip and write "in the costume from @image2 worn as in @imageN". It locks how the costume *actually* renders, which often differs from the sheet.

## 4. Sheet QA checks (pass all before any video)

| Sheet | Must pass | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Hero costume** | Emblem visibly **homemade and asymmetric** (one side short, a notch, wonky edges, hand stitches); **no official logo, oval, printed symbol or symmetric emblem**; archetype readable from silhouette at thumbnail size; mouth area open (unless full mask was chosen); no face on the mannequin | Reroll. A first try often comes back symmetric and official-looking; reroll with "clearly asymmetric, amateur, one wing short" |
| **Hero face sheet** | Same person as the photo (eyes, nose, jaw, hairline, beard); not slimmer, younger or older; no text | Reroll, or use the photo directly |
| **Villain** | **No copied famous villain look** (face paint, smeared smile, green/purple clown palette, cards, breathing mask, tubes, armour plates); the gag property readable; a desi motif | Reroll. Strip any make-up or colour cues that drift toward a famous look |
| **Every person** | **One character only**, four views, no text; a face clearly different from every other sheet | Reroll |
| **Hero vs villain** | Side by side at thumbnail size they can't be confused: different headpiece shape, colour, facial hair and body type | Change the villain's helmet colour, horns, build or facial hair |
| **Child** | Clearly a child of the stated age (not a teen); smiling; no emblem | Reroll |
| **Animal** | Correct leg and horn count; the branding ornament visible; calm; nothing borrowed from another creator's animal character | Reroll |
| **Vehicle** | No lettering, badge or number plate; attached parts present | Reroll |
| **Location plate** | The exact left / centre / right layout; no people, no signboards | Reroll |
| **Whole cast** | **One distinct silhouette and colour per character**; no two characters share a dominant colour; no more than 4–5 faces per clip | Recolour or cut a character |

---

## 5. Reference upload rules

- **Upload order is the numbering.** Hero first: 1 face (photo or sheet), 2 costume sheet, then the look still once you have one.
- **Upload only the sheets that clip uses.** An unused face leaks into shots.
- Higgsfield tags them `@image1…`; Runway uses typed `@Image 1` chips (see `seedance-and-generation.md`).
- **Still describe each character in words** after the tag, so the prompt works even if a tag fails to bind.
- Animals and vehicles don't count as faces, but each extra reference dilutes identity: drop a sheet if identities blur.

## 6. Identity-lock lines (one per reference)

```
Identity lock: the [masked] hero is @image1 ([face source]), [build], [age], not lean, in the costume from @image2 [worn as in @imageN]: [8-word costume summary], [mask state, e.g. mouth and jaw visible, never removed].
[Name], the [6-word look], is @image3; [one feature only he has, e.g. only he has horns].
Do not redesign any face, body or clothing. Every person has exactly two arms and two legs.
```

If the hero and a villain drift toward each other (two masked heads), add the contrast in words: "the hero's ears are black knit, not horns; the villain is clean-shaven, the hero bearded".
