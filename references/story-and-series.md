# Story and series

How to invent an original desi hero, give every episode real stakes and one new toy, build villains and bystanders, and run it as a series that grows.

**Contents**
1. Invent an original hero (archetype substitution)

**Know the gap you're working against.** The strongest series in this genre borrow instant recognition from famous heroes and villains, run on a song the audience already loves, and have an audience primed by earlier episodes. We don't copy logos or other creators' characters, so compensate with **stronger silhouettes, bigger toys, real stakes and a real song**.

## 1. Invent an original hero

**Formula:** `[famous archetype] × [ordinary Indian everyman + trade] × [costume made of household jugaad] × [toy = local animal or vehicle] × [weapon = household object] × [signature track] × [hero colour]`

**Method**
1. **List the archetype's 5 icons:** look, vehicle, weapon, signal, power.
2. **Swap each icon** for the cheapest, most everyday Indian object that does the same job. It must read in one frame with no explanation.
3. **Give him a trade.** It sets his world, his props and later the sponsor category (a cycle mechanic → a cycle-parts sponsor).
4. **Pick one robust visual anchor** the model keeps consistent: a bucket helmet, a knit cap with ears, a gamchha belt. Bold simple shapes beat detailed emblems.
5. **Make the costume homemade and logo-free.** Official logos trip platform IP filters, and homemade is funnier.
6. **Keep the mouth and jaw visible** (cap or mask down to the nose). The deadpan and every mouth gag live there. A full face wrap kills both.
7. **Pick a hero colour that isn't red** (red is another well-known series' signature). Suggest 4: turmeric yellow, indigo, emerald, saffron, rust, teal, magenta, slate.
8. **Name it:** `[desi object or trade word] + [-wala / -man / Bhai / Babu]`, or `[archetype word] + [place]` (Kadhaiwala, Tanker Bhai, Chowkidar Knight). Keep names affectionate, never mocking. The series title on screen is `[NAME] [N]`.

**Archetype substitution bank** (original examples; mix and change them)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 2. Recognition without logos

His audience should name the archetype from a **thumbnail in under 0.5 s**. Use four channels, not a logo:

| Channel | How | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Silhouette** | Exaggerate the archetype's outline: ears, horns, cape, helmet, trident | Two tall stiff cardboard ears on a black knit cap |
| **Colour pair** | The archetype's two colours, in cheap fabric | A faded blue shirt + a red gamchha cape for the flying strongman |
| **Pose / gesture** | The archetype's signature stance, done deadpan | Hands on hips on a water tank; a crouch on a parapet |
| **Homemade emblem** | The archetype's shape cut by hand: **asymmetric**, wonky, stitched, painted with a finger | A bat cut from white cloth, one wing short, red running stitches; never a printed oval |

**Thumbnail test:** shrink the sheet to stamp size. If a stranger can't name the archetype, exaggerate the silhouette or switch archetype.

## 3. Real stakes

"Small and relatable" means **personal, not trivial**. Play the stakes completely straight; the comedy lives in the props, never in the stakes.

| Too trivial | Real (use these) |
|---|---|
| A snack swapped | A widow's pension cash snatched at the post office |
| A lost slipper | A family's land papers stolen by a fake agent |
| Spilled chai | The village's only hand pump padlocked by a water mafia |
| A rude neighbour | A girl's exam admit card torn up the morning of the board exam |
| | A farmer's whole harvest about to be cheated on a rigged scale |
| | A missing goat that is a family's savings |

**Safety:** threats to a child stay **off-screen or symbolic** (a torn admit card, a stolen school bag, a poster). A child is never grabbed, carried off, hurt or shown near a weapon. No animal is shown in danger.

## 4. One new toy per episode

**The toy** is a desi substitute for an iconic element: a vehicle, a gadget or an ally. In the reference series, the episode that introduced the hero's animal vehicle drew **2.8–3.7× the comments** of any other episode, and fans coined names for it. A sequel with no new element lost 53% of its comments.

**Rules**
- **One new toy (or new thing) every episode.** In episode 1 the new thing is the hero himself plus his first toy.
- **Reveal it in parts, never whole before the drop:** an ECU (eye, wheel, bell) → full body in god-rays → **mount/arrival on the drop** → movement details → a frontal charge → the branding ornament ECU (`shot-grammar.md` §5).
- **Put it on the cover** and **name it in the caption**. If fans rename it, use their name next time.
- **Brand it with one ornament** the model keeps (a brass bell, a cloth on one horn, a painted eye, a nazar charm).
- **Make it big.** The toy should fill the frame on its reveal; a gadget needs its own ECU and must cause something big.

**Fresh toy bank:** a camel with a brass bell · a black scooter with a sidecar · an old tractor with a dhol on the bonnet · a water tanker · a school van · a handcart rebuilt with scrap armour · a donkey with saddle bags of tools · a bullock cart with tyre-rubber wheels · an e-rickshaw with a loudspeaker · a giant patchwork kite he hangs under over open fields. Skip elephants and wild animals (welfare and platform filters); farm animals stay calm and unharmed.

## 5. Villains and their intros

**Design rules**
- **Original.** Never a copied famous villain look: no clown face paint, smeared red smile, green hair, playing cards, breathing mask, tubes, luchador mask or armour plates. If a sheet reads as a famous villain, reroll it.
- **A desi motif instead:** a fireworks showman, a fake-ghee trader, a water-tanker mafia boss, a horned dangal champion, a rigged-scale moneylender.
- **A distinct silhouette and colour** from the hero and from every other villain (rust-red helmet vs the hero's black cap; muscular vs a fat henchman).
- **A gag property** on the costume that pays off on impact (an over-oiled turban, a talc-stuffed collar, a bandolier of unlit paper rockets).

**The 5-step intro**
1. **Occlusion:** half-hidden behind an object under a bare bulb (chilli garlands, a hanging lantern).
2. **ECU of the signature feature** before he does anything (a gold tooth, rings, the gag property).
3. **A crime against the vulnerable**, small and real (§3).
4. **The gag property** set up in frame.
5. **A dirty desi trick** that wins him round one (chilli powder, a slippery mustard-oil floor, a borrowed buffalo blocking the lane).

**Henchmen:** 1–2 max per fight, each with one readable prop (a lathi with a taped grip, aviators). More faces = more drift.

## 6. Cast slots

| Slot | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | A deadpan everyman | Never smiles at the joke; mouth visible |
| Toy | The shareable object | One new per episode; cover it |
| Villain | Menace, then slapstick | Intro pattern §5 |
| **Child witness** | The audience's proxy, future sidekick | Recurs silently; only cheers, gasps, runs; never in peril |
| **Indifferent bystander** | Comic chorus | Keeps doing his chore through the fight; cut back 2–3 times as the punchline |
| Straight man | Notices, never acts | A slow-burn reaction |
| **Elder** (Dadi, Nani, headmaster) | Real authority | Ends a fight or outranks the villain; never thrown or hurt |
| Family hero | A relative in the same jugaad suit | Fine as a twist, but **the hero still acts in the climax** |
| Village chorus | Localises every chase | 3–4 one-second tableaux (barber, hand pump, ration queue, tailor) |

## 7. Planted props and payoffs

- **Plant in ECU early, pay off in the same framing later** (a brass bell, a coin, a key, a kite).
- **One running prop crosses episodes** and changes meaning each time (a lure → a gag → a sign of awe → a fake warning).
- **Every episode reuses at least one prop or character** from the last one.
- A plant with no payoff is a broken promise. The Critic checks every plant.

## 8. Serialising

**End-of-episode kit**

| Device | Use when |
|---|---|
| Hero stares into the lens, cut to black | A win |
| "To Be Continued" + a parody "will return in [pun on a famous title]" | A loss or cliffhanger |
| Versus card in both heroes' colours + "Coming Soon" | Setting up a crossover |
| TBC + next-time montage + a post-title stinger introducing a villain | Big event episodes |
| Ironic twist (someone else takes the credit) | A spin-off origin |
| Sincere extreme wide + fade | A rescue episode (often the strongest) |

**Rules**
- **Open the sequel on the last episode's final image**, then pay off its cliffhanger. But the first 0–3 s must still hook a cold viewer: put the incongruity gag first if the final image isn't gripping on its own.
- **Act on comments.** Read the top 5–10 comments of the last episode and fold **one request** into the new one, visibly. Every turn of the reference series answered a comment (a requested song switch, a requested sidekick, a fan's nickname for the toy).
- **Growth ladder** (one step per episode or two): origin vignette → one-location mission → villain arrives, first loss → crossover ally downgraded to small-town economics (invent your own limit: a jetpack that runs on a shared gas cylinder that must be returned) → **the toy** → a family member as hero → meme spin-off → spin-off hero → crossover war → a sidekick born from a fan request → sponsor.
- **Spin-offs as "Episode 0":** a new hero, a new colour, numbering from 0, the title held to the last 5 s, tagged to the parent series. Solo spin-offs pulled about **30%** of a main-series episode, so bring the new hero back **under the main title** fast, as a rival or ally.
- **Crossovers:** two heroes turned against each other by a misunderstanding. **Invent the cause** (both claim the same rescue; a rumour at the chai stall; a mix-up at the tailor's). A flashback gives the stakes weight; end on a versus card.
- **Sponsors:** the product becomes **the hero's own tool in his trade** and never contradicts his powers (a flying hero must not flee on a bicycle). One functional beat + one product ECU slide + one hero shot. Use real product photos for the logo.
- **Cadence:** a post every 2–3 days keeps a series alive.

## 9. The comedy engine

**Tone:** 70–80% straight blockbuster craft, 20–30% desi deflation. The filmmaking never jokes; the objects and situations do. Climaxes are played sincerely.

| # | Gag type | Fresh example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deadpan substitution:** an iconic element replaced by a desi one, never explained | The hero summons his weapon with a pressure-cooker whistle; a stone sil-batta rolls slowly down the lane to his feet |
| 2 | **Epic framing, trivial act** | Low-angle slow-mo crane on the hero catching a roti flipping off the tawa, dust and god-rays |
| 3 | **Ritual in costume** | Masked hero oiling his hair and combing a side-parting before the mission |
| 4 | **Grand entrance, local transaction** | His shared auto won't leave until all 6 seats fill, and the villain waits too |
| 5 | **Interruption delay** (music cut, each a static cutaway) | A baraat band, then a school van, then a man with a gas cylinder on his cycle crosses between them |
| 6 | **Indifferent bystander** | A roadside barber keeps shaving his customer; cut back to him three times |
| 7 | **Real authority wins** | The headmaster walks in; hero and villain both stand in line holding their ears |
| 8 | **Hero humiliated button** | He wins, then his cape snags on barbed wire and tears as he walks off in his vest |
| 9 | **Villain's gag property pays off** | The over-oiled hair makes the first punch slide off; on the second, a cloud of talc puffs out |
| 10 | **Dirty trick reversal** | The villain throws chilli powder; the hero sneezes him across the yard |
| 11 | **Lyric commentary** | A lullaby line lands exactly as the villain is knocked out cold |
| 12 | **Parody on-screen format** | A national-broadcaster "sorry for the interruption" card when the power cuts mid-fight |
| 13 | **Parody caption or title** | "With great power comes a great electricity bill" |
| 14 | **Soundtrack controlled by a character** | A neighbour's wedding DJ starts, and the fight turns into a dance-off on the beat |
| 15 | **Awe reaction** | A kid's ice-gola melts down his wrist, unnoticed, as he stares |
| 16 | **Credit stolen** | He saves the market; next morning the local neta cuts the ribbon in the newspaper photo |
| 17 | **Scale mismatch:** the power used for a chore first | Super-strength pushes a stalled bus out of the mud while every passenger stays seated |

**Tone dials:** *Comedy* = a gag from 1–10 every 10–15 s. *Action* = gags only in the toy and the costume; climax straight. *Emotional* = one sincere stake, a flashback, a switch to a sad classic.

**Never:** dialogue (unless the user overrides; then prefer VO or captions) · the hero winking or laughing at the joke · mocking the village, caste, religion or poverty · graphic violence (no blood, no realistic injury, no weapons used on people; age-restricted posts lose their reach) · fire near people, explosives on bodies, a child or animal in danger.

## 10. Desi setting and trope bank

**Used a lot** (fine, but add a fresh twist): chai-tapri fight · buffalo ride · tomato or sabzi cart smash · Dadi with a chappal or broom · wedding baraat · village mela · railway crossing · sugarcane-field chase · monkey cap or gamchha mask · lathi duel · brick kiln · monsoon rain fight · rooftop chase over drying chillies · river jump.

**Fresh** (less seen; use these first): dhobi ghat with saris drying · a thousand fort steps at dawn · river-ghat steps with boats · ration-shop queue · weekly haat · a power cut at night with one inverter bulb · school sports-day tug-of-war · kite-festival rooftops (cotton thread only) · water-tanker queue · stepwell · salt pans · tea-garden slopes · gully-cricket match · state-bus stand · cattle fair (animals calm) · tent-house godown · photo studio with painted backdrops · a cyber café · a flour mill (chakki) in white dust · a tailor's shop with hanging cloth · a village post office · a toll plaza on a highway.

**Keep out:** religious ceremonies as gag targets, political rallies, real brands without a sponsor deal, real public figures.

## 11. Series bible template

```
SERIES: [name] | COLOUR: [one hue, not red by default] | NUMBERING: [main 1..n; spin-offs from 0]
SIGNATURE TRACK: [user's nostalgic song, added in-app] | SECOND TRACKS: [sad / action]
HERO: [trade] [age/build] [costume pieces, each a household object] [robust anchor] [mask: mouth visible?]
ARCHETYPE RIFF: [archetype] → silhouette [x], colour pair [x], pose [x], emblem [homemade, asymmetric]
TOY: [animal or vehicle] [one branding ornament] [reveal order]
WEAPON / SIGNAL / POWER: [object] / [object] / [ordinary act filmed as a power]
CAST: villain [motif + gag property] | child witness | bystander | straight man | elder
WORLD: [village/town] [3 signature locations] [time-of-day palette]
RUNNING PROPS: [1–3 callbacks]
IDENT: [own concept, ≤10% runtime] | COVER: [own numeral style + colour]
OPEN THREADS: [cliffhanger, comment requests, next toy]
```
